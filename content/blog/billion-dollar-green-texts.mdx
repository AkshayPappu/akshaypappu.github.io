Recently, one of my friends was on vacation internationally, and I sent him a text.

I was immediately hit with the dreaded green bubble. No confirmation of delivery, no smooth animation, just green. As an iPhone user, this was one of the most frustrating experiences of the week.

But then it got me thinking... What causes texts to turn green in the first place? I did a little bit of research, and it turns out, the reason is a lot more interesting than you'd expect.

It comes down to 2 fundamental messaging services: **SMS** and **iMessage**.

## What is SMS and why was it invented?

In the late 1980s, engineers wanted to find a way to send small bits of text over a network instead of having to make voice calls all the time. To do this, they took advantage of how cellular networks are structured.

Cellular networks are split into two layers:

**Data Layer:**
- Carries out actual user content (e.g. voice during a call or internet data) and requires a continuous connection

**Control Layer:**
- Runs in the background and coordinates everything: setting up calls, routing devices to cell towers, and managing connections across the network

Because the control layer is always active and handles only small amounts of data, engineers designed SMS to package text into compact messages and transmit them through this signaling system.

As cell phones exploded in popularity in the late 90's and 2000's, SMS became the go-to protocol for sending text messages.

## Why did Apple invent iMessage?

After the iPhone was released, the demands of text messaging increased significantly and SMS was not able to keep up.

A huge limitation of SMS was that its features were dependent on existing telecom infrastructure, which limited message sizes, media quality, and international texting costs.

Apple decided to create iMessage to modernize messaging in the age of the internet.

Instead of being routed through carrier networks, messages sent from an iPhone would be routed over the internet through Apple's internal servers enabling Apple to directly control the fundamental logic of messaging (hence the blue text bubbles).

## Why are my texts green?

So coming back to the question you came here for.

##### iPhone → Android

When sending texts from an iPhone to an Android, Apple defaults to SMS since Android phones are not registered in Apple's iMessage directory. The message never touches Apple's servers — it gets routed through the carrier network like a text from 2005.

This is why the group chat "breaks" when you add that one Android friend.

##### iPhone → iPhone (my case)

However, as in my case, iPhone to iPhone texts can also sometimes be green.

Since iMessage sends messages over the internet, if either the sender or recipient cannot connect to Apple's servers, like when you're on vacation internationally with spotty data, iMessage fails and resorts to SMS.

## The billion-dollar reason

At first glance, the difference between blue and green messages seems like a small design choice.

**It isn't.**

The color of your messages is one of the most effective forms of product lock-in ever created.

If all of your friends use iMessage, the experience is seamless. But the moment even one person isn't on it, the entire conversation degrades.

By keeping iMessage exclusive, Apple ensures that iPhones work best with other iPhones, making it harder for users to switch away.

> Nobody wants to be the reason the chat turns green.
